Solar Rig Wiring

Complete connections for the single-ESP32 tracker: 5 V adapter for logic and servos, solar panel measured through the INA219 into a test load. Wire it in the order of the list, ticking each one off.

Labels like 5V, 3V3 and the ground symbol mean "connect to that rail". Every ground symbol is the same common ground, and that includes panel −. Pin order shown is the common DOIT ESP32 DevKit V1 (30-pin); check your board's printed labels, as some clones swap positions.

Bring-up order

  1. Rails only. Wires 1–4 and 6, nothing else plugged in. Measure 4.9–5.2 V on the 5 V rail and 3.2–3.4 V on the 3V3 rail.
  2. ESP32 alone. Upload a blink sketch and confirm it boots from the adapter.
  3. INA219. Wires 7–10. Run an I²C scanner; it should find 0x40.
  4. DS18B20. Wires 22–25. Read the temperature and warm the probe in your hand.
  5. LDRs. Wires 16–21. Print both ADC values; covering one should drop its number sharply.
  6. Capacitor, then servos. Wire 5 first, then 26–28. Sweep the tilt servo. Then 29–31 for the wiper.
  7. Panel last. Wires 11–15 in sunlight. Compare the INA219 voltage against a multimeter across the load.

Watch out for

  • Panel − to ground (wire 15). Without it the INA219 reads current but the voltage is garbage.
  • Servo power. Servo red wires go to the 5 V rail, never the ESP32's 3V3 pin. Missing the capacitor causes random resets when servos move.
  • USB plus adapter. When uploading over USB, unplug wire 3 unless your board has a diode on its USB 5 V line.
  • Hot load resistor. Around 9 W in full sun. Mount it on metal, off the breadboard and away from wires.
  • Moving cables. LDR, DS18B20 and wiper leads ride on the tilting panel. Leave a slack loop at the axle for ±60° travel.
  • Breadboard width. The DevKit covers most of one breadboard. Use two side by side.
